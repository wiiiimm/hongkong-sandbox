HONG KONG SANDBOX / ASTRA

A city you can step into.

Explore mapped buildings across all 18 Hong Kong districts, including Lantau villages, Mui Wo, Tai O, Cheung Chau and Peng Chau. Nearby city sections load as you travel. If a section cannot load, movement waits for its buildings and Retry remains available.

Building coverage combines official Lands Department outlines with OpenStreetMap names, uses and supplementary geometry. Every retained official building record across Hong Kong is included, including temporary, open-sided and unnamed structures. Some source forms have multiple polygon components. Around Pak Ngan Heung, verified official 3D models replace matching extrusions and preserve detailed roofs. Government BaseHeight and TopHeight are approximate absolute elevations above Hong Kong Principal Datum; their valid difference sets building height. Missing heights remain labelled estimates. Building forms are simplified and façades are illustrative.

Every building follows the city clock: windows glow in the evening, gradually switch off overnight and begin waking after 4 am. Homes fill up around 9 pm, peak at 10 pm and begin sleeping around 11 pm. Offices thin out from 6 pm, with some working through 8 pm and scattered lights past midnight. Shops light up gradually at dusk, begin closing around 9 pm and mostly close by 11 pm, with some remaining open after midnight. The city is quietest at 4 am. Mapped uses and researched mixed-use buildings guide the patterns. Nearby windows remain steady; distant lights have subtle atmospheric shimmer, which can be switched off and respects reduced-motion settings. Street lighting stays on. Drag the circular 24-hour clock, enter an exact time, or use night shortcuts and time lapse (one hour every eight seconds). Window occupancy is simulated. The sky follows the selected Hong Kong date and time, with seasonal sunrise and sunset.

The terrain comes from the project’s Lands Department DTM, sampled at 70 metres, with the retained official 5 metre DTM around Mui Wo. Everything uses metres with no vertical exaggeration. Ground detail, footbridges and building interiors are not yet a street-level survey. Coverage and local accuracy vary; imported buildings are not a complete architectural survey. Recent reclamation may differ from the older terrain surface.

The Footbridges layer renders mapped elevated pedestrian spans and building connections with decks, rails and explicitly mapped covers. Click a deck to inspect its source and height assumptions. Missing deck elevations and widths are estimated; ordinary indoor corridors and unsupported ground approaches are omitted. Elevated walking and building entrances remain separate integration work.

Local detail adds mapped beaches, piers, public spaces, pitches and airport aprons. Their outlines come from OpenStreetMap; colours and terrain-draped elevations are illustrative. A visible pier or apron does not establish public walking access. Some remote or restricted places offer aerial views only.

Walk with WASD, drag to look, Shift to run and C to switch camera. In flight, W/S climb and descend, A/D turn, Shift accelerates and C switches camera. Esc returns to Explore. Touch controls are also available.

Use the Sky and Weather tabs for dated sun/moon positions, stargazing, live Hong Kong time, live HKO observations and manual weather. The original game retains additional features whose restoration is still planned, including full aircraft/gameplay modes, tides, radar and typhoon controls. Shooting-star controls, manual lightning/thunder and optional weather sound reuse the original game. Sound begins only when you enable it; Reduced Motion suppresses meteor trails and lightning flashes.
